# Rebalancing: Automated and Manual Operations

<Term slug="rebalancing">Rebalancing</Term> is the operational work of
moving partition ownership between nodes in response to nodes being added
or removed, or to data volume and access frequency drifting out of
balance. No matter how carefully a partitioning scheme is designed, any
system that runs long enough will eventually need rebalancing.

## Why rebalancing becomes necessary

Three triggers show up most often:

- **Adding a node**: to grow processing capacity or storage, a new node
  gets added, and some of the partitions previously owned by existing
  nodes need to move to it
- **Removing a node**: whether due to a failure or a planned scale-down,
  the partitions that node owned need to be reassigned to the remaining
  nodes
- **Skew in data or load**: a split that started out even can drift as
  data grows or access patterns shift — the hotspot problem from the
  previous lesson is one flavor of this

## Keeping the number of moved keys small

The thing to avoid above all in rebalancing is having nearly the entire
dataset move every time a single node is added or removed — which is
exactly what happens with a naive modulo-based hash split.

Consistent hashing (two lessons back) was designed precisely to confine
that movement to roughly one node's worth of the ring. Ideally, going from
n nodes to n+1 nodes should move on the order of 1/(n+1) of all keys.
Increasing the number of virtual nodes helps spread that movement across
many nodes rather than concentrating it on a small handful — the same
behavior you could observe in the visualization in lesson 2.

## Automated vs. manual rebalancing

Who decides when and how much to rebalance splits operational styles into
two broad categories.

**Automated rebalancing**
The system detects a change in node count or a load imbalance and
triggers partition reassignment on its own once a threshold is crossed.
This reduces operator effort, but a false detection or unlucky timing can
trigger a large, unplanned data shift during a period when it hurts
performance the most.

**Manual rebalancing**
An operator explicitly triggers reassignment. The system's role is limited
to surfacing how skewed things currently are; a human decides whether and
when to act. This avoids surprise movement at inconvenient times, but adds
a human cost to the decision and the work itself, and response can lag.

<Callout type="tip">
  In practice, many teams land on something in between: monitoring runs
  automatically, but the actual reassignment trigger requires operator
  approval before it executes. Rather than an all-automatic-or-all-manual
  choice, it's better framed as a question of how much you hand off to the
  system.
</Callout>

## Summary

This module covered two foundational schemes — key-range partitioning and
hash partitioning — their evolution into consistent hashing with virtual
nodes, the hotspot problem that no partitioning scheme alone can solve,
and the rebalancing operations needed as nodes come and go. These aren't
independent techniques; they're a toolkit for one underlying question: how
do you split data and load across many nodes, and keep that split healthy
over time?
